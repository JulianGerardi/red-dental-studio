Overview

A kitchen that feeds whole offices, one tray at a time.

GRILL is a kitchen in Mercedes, Buenos Aires that delivers lunch to companies. Each company covers a daily amount per employee and pays at the end of the month; each employee chooses what they eat.

I designed both sides of the service and built them as working web apps with an AI-assisted workflow: GRILL Empresas for employees and GRILL Team, the panel the kitchen runs the day from.

The problem

Everything lived on paper and in WhatsApp.

Kitchen tickets, orders per company, new employee accounts and the monthly account closing. Every day the team gathers choices, counts dishes for the kitchen, labels trays, plans the delivery run and keeps each company’s account, all before 11:30.

Make ordering take less than a minute, and turn the kitchen’s morning into a checklist.

Two users2 apps

Same orders, two very different mornings.

Employee · phone

A minute between meetings, before the cutoff. Needs to know when the next delivery is, what the kitchen suggests today, how much the company covers, and how to pick a side dish.

Kitchen team · desktop

The whole morning, hands busy. Needs how many of each dish to cook, a label for every tray, the delivery run by company, and what to bill each one.

Employee app

Order lunch in a few taps.

The cutoff comes first

The screen opens on the next delivery date and a countdown to the 10:30 cutoff. When today’s cutoff has passed, a banner says the order goes to the next delivery and can be changed until 10:30 that day. No dead end.

  • A short selection first: the kitchen picks a few dishes each day, and the full menu is one link away.
  • The allowance is always visible: “Your company covers up to $11,000 a day”.

Choosing is one step

Tapping a dish opens a sheet to pick the side dish and leave a note for the kitchen, up to 140 characters. The button carries the price, “Agregar · $10.800”, so there is no surprise at checkout.

The full menu has 59 dishes in 9 categories, filtered by gluten-free (Sin TACC) and vegetarian.

History and dark mode

My orders shows the week or the month day by day, with a summary to download. The whole app has a dark theme, and the copy talks like people in Mercedes do: “Elegí”, “Tocá”, “¿La olvidaste?”.

Kitchen panel

The kitchen’s morning, as a checklist.

Summary of the day

Units, orders and companies at the top, with the departure time. Below, one card per job of the morning (kitchen, labels, delivery) and the alerts still to resolve, which are marked from the bell.

Kitchen ticket

Every order is added up by dish and side, grouped by category, with a checkbox to tick as each one is cooked. It prints as the day’s ticket.

Labels that fit the real sheet

Each tray gets a label on a sheet of 70 × 25.4 mm labels. “Start from” lets the team reuse a half-used sheet by tapping the first free cell, and a note reminds them to print at real size so labels don’t shift off the die cut.

Delivery, orders and accounts

  • Delivery: stops by company, trays per person and a checklist per stop.
  • Orders: totals to invoice for any period, units dispatched and status (new, in preparation, delivered).
  • Companies and employees: each company gets a registration code; the team can block accounts or make someone an admin.
Access

Two doors, each one explained.

Employees don’t sign up on their own: their company creates the account. The login says so, tells them who to ask, and offers companies a way to request a proposal. The kitchen has its own internal entrance, with a link back for employees who land there by mistake.
